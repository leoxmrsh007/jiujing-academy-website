  "Rites" (礼) are often mistaken today for empty formality, but their original sense is "order" — setting proper measure among human relations, so that closeness and distance, seniority and station each find their place without friction. "Music" (乐) supplies what rites alone cannot: harmony, dissolving the stiffness order can otherwise produce.
  
  This third lecture focuses on the coming-of-age and mourning rites described in the Yili and Liji, examining how the ancients used ritual to hold the two hardest passages of a life: the weight of adulthood and the grief of death. 「礼」常被今人误解为繁文缛节，殊不知其本义为「序」——为人伦关系划定分寸，使亲疏、尊卑、内外各得其位而不相扰。「乐」则补礼之不足，以「和」消解序中可能生出的疏离与生硬。

第三讲聚焦《仪礼》与《礼记》中论「丧礼」「冠礼」的篇章，探讨古人如何借由仪式，处理人生中最难安顿的两桩大事：成年之责任与死亡之哀恸。仪式并非形式主义，而是一套帮助情感有序流动的容器。
