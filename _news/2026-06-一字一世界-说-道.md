body_en: |
  The character 道 combines 辵 (walking) and 首 (head). Together, 道 originally depicted not an abstract set of rules but a concrete act — discerning direction while walking. This is the first clue the character's makers left us: the Dao was never something thought up while sitting still.
  
  In bronze script, 道 still shows a human figure interwoven with a road, highly pictorial; by seal script the strokes have become linear and abstract; by regular script it is fully symbolic, the trace of walking almost illegible. The form's evolution itself mirrors the Dao's historical journey from concrete practice to abstract concept.
  
  Reading the Tao Te Ching's opening — "the Dao that can be spoken is not the constant Dao" — as pure metaphysical speculation risks empty abstraction. Remembering the "walking" hidden inside the character reminds us this is first a practical warning: the true Dao is discerned only while walking it; what is spoken is already its trace.

「道」字从「辵」从「首」——辵是行走之意，首是头。合而观之，道最初描绘的不是一套抽象规则，而是「行走中辨认方向」这一具体动作。这是汉字造字者留给我们的第一层提示：道，从来不是坐在原地想出来的。

金文中的「道」，字形尚见人形与道路交织，图画感极强；至小篆，笔画趋于线性抽象；至楷书，已完全符号化，行走的痕迹几乎不可辨。字形演变本身，恰好隐喻了「道」从具体践行到抽象概念的历史进程。

读《道德经》「道可道，非常道」，若只从字面的哲学思辨去理解，容易滑入纯粹形上学的空谈。若记得「道」字里藏着的「行走」二字，便知这句话首先是一句实践的提醒：真正的道，唯有在行走中才被辨认，说出来的，已是它的痕迹。
