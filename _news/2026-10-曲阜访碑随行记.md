body_en: |
  I barely slept the night before departure — not from excitement, but nerves. The list of steles to read over three days ran two full pages, and I worried I wouldn't recognize half the archaic characters. Only on site did I realize that recognizing characters is the shallowest layer — the real difficulty is standing before a stele and imagining the state of mind of the person who carved it.
  
  A fellow student pointed at a worn stele and said, "look, this stroke breaks off — not from weathering, the carver's hand slipped that day." In that moment I understood: a stele records not just text, but the real breath and hesitation of the person who wrote and carved it.
  
  By the third day my notebook was full and my feet ached, but my mind felt steadier than when we set out. On the bus back, almost no one spoke — everyone just looked out the window. Perhaps that was the best possible ending to the trip.

出发前一晚几乎没睡好——不是兴奋，是紧张。三天要读的碑文清单有整整两页，我担心自己认不出几个古体字。到了现场才发现，认字只是最外层的功夫，真正难的是站在碑前，想象刻字之人当年的心境。

同行的一位师兄指着一方残碑说：「你看，这一笔断了，不是风化，是当年刻工手抖了一下。」那一刻我忽然明白，碑刻记录的从来不只是文字内容，还有写字与刻字之人那一瞬间真实的呼吸与犹豫。

三天下来，笔记本记满了，脚也走酸了，但心里比出发前更定。回程大巴上，大家很少说话，各自望着窗外——这大概是这趟游学最好的结尾。
