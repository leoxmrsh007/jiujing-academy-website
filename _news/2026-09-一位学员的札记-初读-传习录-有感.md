body_en: |
  Before joining the academy I thought "the unity of knowing and acting" was a motivational slogan — know it, then do it. Only reading Wang Yangming's own words did I realize I'd misunderstood for half a lifetime: he wasn't saying "know first, then act," but that knowing already IS a part of acting — the two were never separate; the separation is an illusion we construct after the fact.
  
  The first time I was asked, in a shared-study session, "what are you thinking right now," I froze — I realized I almost never notice my own thoughts; I'm simply pushed along by them. That single question has stayed with me all these months.
  
  I can't yet claim to have unified knowing and acting, but I've at least learned to pause before impulse, and glance at what's happening inside. That, I think, is where cultivation begins.

入院之前，我以为「知行合一」是一句励志口号——知道了就要做到。读到阳明先生原文才发现，误会了整整半生：他讲的不是「先知后行」，而是「知」本身就已经是「行」的一部分，二者从未分离过，分离只是我们后天想象出来的假象。

第一次在书院共修课上被问到「你此刻在想什么」，我愣住了——原来我平时几乎从不觉察自己的念头，只是被念头推着走。这一问，成了我这半年最常回想的一句话。

还没能说自己「知行合一」，但至少学会了在冲动之前，先停一停，看一眼自己心里正在发生什么。这大概就是修行的起点。
